## 1. The ruling in one paragraph

**The comb rotation is the product.** A comb is a closed, invite-joined group capped
at 20 people that collectively fills one hive for one member at a time — *"this month
the comb is writing for Sarah"* — seals it on her birthday, blooms it for her, and
rotates. Private hives remain, as the **personal mode**. The comb is the hero: it is
the retention engine, the viral loop, and the thing we sell. **A comb costs $5.99 per
month, paid by the organizer.** Everyone else participates free.

This is not a new invention. It is `POLLINATE_V2_SPEC.md` §18.2, promoted from
"Cycle 11–12, Project 18" to the centre of the product, and repriced.

## 2. Why the shape changed (the reasoning, so it can be checked)

Three problems in the pre-ruling model, all real, all closed by the same move:

**P1 — The retention thesis rested on willpower and had no evidence.** The model
assumed a person writes about their child weekly for years. Nothing in the repo or
the workspace measures whether anyone does. Rotation **removes willpower from the
loop**: other people's occasions set the cadence. In a comb of 12 you write eleven
times a year and receive once — roughly monthly engagement driven by a calendar.

**P2 — The network effect was claimed but not present.** A 1:1 private hive whose
recipient may never install is a referral loop, not a network effect. A rotating comb
is **N-to-1, recurring, and multi-party**. §18.1 already says it: *"the only viral
loop that doesn't require a feed."* One seeded comb of 20 produces 20 reveals a year
and 20 people who have each written for someone else.

**P3 — The paywall could not fire.** The delivery meter charged on a user's *second*
delivery, with the first free forever. For most users that event does not occur
within twelve months of install, so a cohort produced approximately zero year-one
revenue **by construction**. §17.5.2b already conceded the flagship 18-year use case
pays $0 across eighteen years. A monthly comb subscription fires every month a group
is active.

## 4. The revenue model

### 4.1 The line

| Line | Ruled |
|---|---|
| **Comb plan** | **$5.99 / month**, auto-renewing, via Apple IAP. Bought by the comb owner. |
| **What it buys** | Running a comb: rotations, the invite link, up to 20 members, the collective seal + reveal, rotation notifications, and the comb pot. |
| **What it does not buy** | Membership. Joining a comb, writing into one, and receiving a reveal are **free forever, on every tier.** |
| **First rotation** | Free (D2). The subscription starts when the second rotation opens. |
| **Personal hives, entries, export, seeds, review cadence, zaps** | Unchanged and free — §17.5.2a stands. |
| **Ads** | Never. Unchanged. |

**Why this does not violate "never meter the graph" (§17.5.2a).** The principle bars
charging for *connection*. The comb plan charges for the **rotation engine** —
scheduling, the collective sealed volume, the reveal, the notification fabric, the
pot. Members connect and write for free; the ritual's orchestration is the artifact.
Stated explicitly here so nobody re-litigates it as a contradiction.

### 4.2 Why monthly is defensible now, when §17.5.1 ruled it out

§17.5.1 ruled: *"Nobody in this category sells monthly"* — because **Pollinate's value
is episodic, enormous at the reveal and quiet for eleven months**, and a monthly bill
churns during the quiet stretch.

**That reasoning was correct, and its premise no longer holds.** The premise was
annual, episodic value. A comb rotating monthly delivers a reveal *every month* — a
different member each time. The quiet stretch the ruling was protecting against does
not exist inside an active comb. The ruling is superseded by the change in the
underlying product, not overturned on its merits.

**The corollary is a warning, not a footnote:** monthly pricing is only defensible
while rotations actually happen monthly. **A comb that stops rotating is a comb that
churns next month.** Rotation cadence and subscription retention are the same number.
This is why condition C1 in §6 is the number that decides the business.

### 4.3 O1 — my recommendation on individual Plus

**Retire the $39.99/yr delivery-metered plan. Ship one paid line.**

- It cannot fire in year one for most cohorts (P3 above), and §17.5.2b already
  conceded the flagship use case pays $0 under it regardless.
- Two paid lines at launch doubles the IAP surface, the paywall copy, the entitlement
  logic and the support burden, for a line we have argued does not work.
- Simplicity at the store page: *"Pollinate is free. A comb is $5.99 a month."*

**The alternative** — keep both — is defensible only if we believe a meaningful cohort
of solo multi-hive users will pay without ever joining a comb. We have no data either
way, and D4's logic applies: it is trivial to add a line later, expensive to remove one.
**Recommend retiring; awaiting Colin.** `ENG-76` should not start until this is ruled.

### 4.4 The math, stated honestly

$5.99/mo = **$71.88 per comb-year** at twelve-month retention. Net of Apple's 15%
(Small Business Program, `OPS-7`) ≈ **$61.10**.

| Target | Combs required (gross) | Engaged users @ 12/comb |
|---|---|---|
| $100K gross | ~1,400 active combs | ~17K |
| $500K gross | ~6,960 active combs | ~84K |
| **$1M gross** | **~13,900 active combs** | **~167K** |

Read "active combs" as a **steady-state** count, not a cumulative one. At 5% monthly
churn, holding 13,900 active combs requires adding **~700 new paying combs per month**
at steady state.

**The tradeoff against the $99/yr alternative, stated plainly so it is not discovered
later:** $99/yr reaches $1M at ~10,100 combs; $5.99/mo needs **~38% more combs** for
the same gross, and adds monthly churn exposure. Against that, $5.99 is an impulse
decision where $99 is a deliberation, and cold start is the real risk — the same
argument §17.5.2 used to accept a *higher* price is the argument for a *lower* one
here, because the binding constraint has moved from anchoring to activation.
**Colin ruled $5.99/mo; this section records the cost of that choice, not an objection.**

Compare the superseded individual model: $1M gross required **25,006 subscribers** at
$39.99/yr, which at 1–4% install→paid is 625K–1.7M installs. **~13,900 combs is a
materially smaller thing to go build.**

**And say the honest thing:** $1M/yr is a top-decile outcome for a consumer app on a
2–3 year arc. The comb shape is the version worth betting on, not a guarantee.

### 4.5 A ratified constraint Colin has overridden — recorded, not hidden

`POLLINATE_V2_ASSIGNMENTS.md` §11 lists: *"Any price change before `ENG-78` has
reveal→signup data"* under **Do not start.** This ruling is a price change made
without that data. Colin's ruling supersedes the constraint.

**The constraint's rationale still binds every future change:** do not answer a bad
funnel with a price lever. If the comb plan converts badly, the diagnosis order is
rotation participation (C1) → reveal quality (`DES-17`/`DES-21`) → price, in that
order. Price is the last knob, not the first.

## 5. What this means for the product surface

### 5.1 Positioning

The §0 lean-in / lean-out table stands. One row is **added**, not changed:

| Lean IN | Lean OUT |
|---|---|
| **The comb — a closed group writing for one member at a time** | **"Community," "group feed," "social network," "post"** |

**Community without a feed.** Colin asked for "a community around lifting others up."
§0 rules that *"a public gratitude feed kills this."* These are not in conflict and
must not be recorded as a compromise between them: a comb is **closed, capped at 20,
and writes rather than posts**, and co-authors cannot see each other's entries until
the volume seals (§18.1). Nobody performs, because there is no audience — only a
subject. That is the resolution.

**Consequence: cut the friend feed.** §18.2's own caution — *"a friend feed is the
weakest asset in the app and it competes for the attention hives need"* — is now
binding, not advisory. The Hive tab becomes comb-first.

### 5.2 The bitcoin layer

Unchanged in mechanism, re-ranked in emphasis:

1. **The comb pot (`ENG-66`, spec §5.2d) is the flagship nectar feature.** Eleven
   friends dropping nectar into Sarah's birthday hive is group gifting — a large real
   behaviour with genuinely poor incumbent UX — wrapped in something people want to
   open. **Guardrail G2 is binding and unchanged:** each contribution settles
   direct-to-recipient at contribution time; the pot is a *display over ledger rows*.
   A pooled balance is a licensing event.
2. **The honeycomb-as-wallet (`ENG-65`, §5.2b)** stays — the best argument the bitcoin
   layer is native rather than bolted on.
3. **Time-locked volumes (19d, §5.2e)** drop from flagship to long-tail story. Still
   the most novel idea in the plan; still option (b) or nothing (G4); still a social
   lock that the UI must describe honestly.
4. **19a-first phasing is unchanged and remains the best call in the document.** Build
   the comb pot against simulated nectar. Do not reorder.

**Unresolved contradiction, still owed a ruling (carried from the V2 spec):** §5.5(4)
rules *"no projected returns, ever,"* while §5.2(e)'s pitch line — *"$50 in 2026
versus 50,000 sats in 2044"* — is a projected-return claim. Apple 3.1.1 and
FTC-adjacent risk both bite. This ruling does not resolve it; it stays open.

## 6. The four conditions that decide this

None are knowable today: there is no analytics SDK in the build and no external
cohort. **Seed three real combs — a run club, a small group, a group chat — instrument
these four, and the answer arrives in eight weeks.**

| # | Condition | Threshold | Instrumented by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** | **Rotation participation** — share of a ~12-member comb who write for that month's subject | **≥60%, sustained 3 months** | `ENG-89` |
| **C2** | Reveal→install for non-member recipients | ≥25% | `ENG-78` (exists) |
| **C3** | Comb survival — seeded combs still rotating at month 6 | ≥50% | `ENG-89` |
| **C4** | Organizer willingness to pay at the comb level | Conversion at the first paid rotation | `ENG-89` |

> **C1 is the number that decides the business.** If it returns 20%, no repricing and
> no design pass saves the model, and we should learn that before building the paid
> tier — not after. C1 and subscription retention are the same number (§4.2).

**This is why the analytics contradiction must be closed first.** `src/constants/legalCopy.js`
(:159, :207) currently promises *"There is no analytics, crash-reporting or tracking
code in it either."* That is a published claim that forecloses C1–C4 permanently. V2
§20.2 already prescribes the fix — **narrow the promise, do not delete it** — and it
must be done **before** the policy is published, not after. `OPS-8`.
